# Independent turnaround review

You supervise a fictional volunteer distribution team lending repair kits. The published report says the team prepared 24 kits before the change and 24 after it. Confirmed requests were also 24 in both windows. Handoffs fell from 20 to 14. No data support treating the drop as an established effect of the new rule. Use the [case data](../data/windows.json) and the five chapters to recommend one reversible improvement.

## Submit a short operating brief

1. Name the outcome, observation window and denominator. Calculate the drops in count and share. Identify at least two stakeholders who would learn different things from the preparation and handoff counts.
2. Draw the four stages and name a legitimate decision owner for each. Separate the inspector's evidence from authority to release. Explain what happens when the release owner is absent and nobody authorized is available.
3. Compare a preparation target with a safe fill target. Predict one measurable perverse response to each, and identify evidence that could contradict you.
4. Calculate ceilings for the changed window, the extra signer, and the failed inspection batch. Report when preparation, inspection, release and counter staffing would matter. Name one reason the numerical ceiling might not be reached.
5. Write a two-window trial with comparison conditions, adoption and outcome measures, a safety stop and an explicit continuation rule. State how you would explain a missed target after a real capacity shock. Do not recruit volunteers or make operational changes for this exercise.

## How reviewers will judge it

Give equal weight to clear outcome framing, decision ownership, incentive risks, capacity evidence, and a reversible revision rule. Safety bypass is a substantive error, even when it raises the handoff count. A strong answer distinguishes observations, modeled ceilings, and counterfactual claims.

Do the brief unaided before using the [review key](turnaround-key.md). If you ask an AI for a critique, use only the synthetic scenario, retain your initial decision and independently recompute the abnormal shift. State one suggestion you accepted or rejected and why. Then answer without assistance: four eligible previously inspected kits arrive after an inspection failure. Which stage can still bind, and what has to be confirmed before counting those kits?
